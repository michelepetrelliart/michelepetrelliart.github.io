4340 Stale Bread
The scene unfolds within an oppressive darkness, devoid of any real shelter, where three figures worn down by time and hunger consume their only available sustenance: pieces of hard, dry bread. The core of the work lies in the ruthless contrast between material destitution and the iron will to preserve human dignity. The white tablecloth, meticulously spread out on the bare floor, represents the final bulwark of civilization and self-respect. Despite the total deprivation of a roof and the evident malnutrition etched into their hollow faces, the protagonists insist on setting the table as if they were in a comfortable domestic setting, refusing to surrender to the total devaluation of their condition. This depiction mirrors a systemic fracture: on one side, a growing indigence silently spreading across Europe and the world; on the other, an increasingly extreme concentration of wealth in the hands of privileged classes capable of steering governments, conditioning elections, and manipulating public opinion through propaganda. Hard bread thus becomes the tangible symbol of structural inequality, where the survival of the many sustains the economic and political dominance of the few.
